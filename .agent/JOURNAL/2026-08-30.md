# 2026-08-30

## What this pass built

The working system itself: `.agent/`, a standing prompt with no dated fact in
it, a state file nobody writes by hand, a queue, and this journal. No file under
`src/` changed.

## Why

A handoff written on 2026-08-27 opened a session on 2026-08-29 with four claims
about the repository. All four were wrong: it said the tree was clean (thirteen
modified files), that HEAD was on `196b441` (five commits further on), that the
version was 2.8.0 (2.8.1), and that the offline gates ran in series and
parallelising them was the first untouched opportunity (`run-gates.mjs` had been
pooling one worker per core, with a `--jobs` flag, all along).

The lesson is not that the author was careless. It is that prose about a
repository decays at the speed the repository moves, and that a frame and a
state cannot live in the same file. So `PROMPT.md` is forbidden from containing
a version, a commit, a count or a current open point, and `ETAT.json` is
forbidden from being edited.

## Pass twelve: what the manifest was handing to the site

Reading the weight gate's target led somewhere else. `dist/assets/content.js` is
228,406 bytes and the gate calls it the one cost every reader pays. Checking
that claim meant checking whether the content script pulls anything else at
runtime, since `web_accessible_resources` listed four ESM chunks.

It does not. Zero `getURL`, zero `chrome-extension`, zero dynamic import, zero
icon reference, and all 22 `url()` in the stylesheet are inline `data:` SVG.
`scripts/bundle-content.ts` re-bundles the content script as a self-contained
classic IIFE precisely so nothing is fetched at runtime, and repoints the
manifest at it. The chunks crxjs exposed were for the loader that step replaces.

So six resources were reachable from any kick.com page for no reason, at stable
URLs, which is a reliable test for whether the extension is installed. That is
worth more here than in most extensions, since this site already walls off what
it detects. The source manifest declared two of the six; both are gone, and
`patchManifest` drops whatever crxjs adds. Chrome and Firefox builds both come
out with none.

One correction on the way. A first reading reported `content.js` at 200,874 and
called it a 12 percent drop from the gate's reference of 228,460, unnoticed
because the gate only fails upward. That was a character count against a byte

## Pass fifteen: a real bug, found behind four wrong accusations

The plan was to make `live-recycle`'s central check runnable offline by
provoking the recycling instead of waiting for a busy chat to do it. Getting
there took four probes that accused working code, and the product was right
every time.

**One.** The row driver rewrote every previously placed row on each step, which
destroys what the extension inserted into them. It reported one translation in
eight while the engine had been called eight times.

**Two.** The mock read a single `q` and returned a single segment. Google's
adapter joins a batch with newlines into one `q` and splits the answer by lines,
so every line after the first came back identical to its original. The product
dropped them and said so on the line, in words: "the translation came back the
same as the original". I read that message, wrote a fix for multiple `q`
parameters, and it was still the wrong shape.

**Three.** Reading the skip reason off the row rather than off the injection
target returned null, and I took a missing attribute for a missing decision.
That produced a confident finding, written down mid-pass, that bursts of chat
messages were being silently dropped. With the mock finally faithful, phase one
of this gate translates nine rows out of nine. There is no burst defect. The
claim was mine, not the product's.

**Four.** The first witness for the burst hypothesis exited 1 on a Playwright
timeout rather than on the assertion, because removing the filter clear also
hid the element clicked next.

**What was actually there.** Kick's virtual scroller reuses a row by replacing
its contents. The row is then the mutation TARGET and never an added node, and
the observer collected candidates from added nodes and their descendants only.
Eight recycled rows: no translation, no reason on the line, no provider call.
The comment sitting directly above that loop says the recycling case is covered
by watching childList with subtree, which is true of the events and false of the
handling.

`enclosingMessageRow` walks up from the mutation target. Re-processing a row
whose text has not changed costs nothing, because `process` returns on the id
the row already carries, which is also what stops our own insertions from
looping.

Two witnesses. The gate goes from 9 of 9 to 1 of 9 with the walk removed. And a
unit test now fails without it, which required the test double to stop throwing
away the `MutationObserver` callback it was handed: no test could deliver a
mutation, so nothing in 620 tests could reach that branch at all.

The gate's own assertion needed hardening too. It failed only at zero and
therefore passed on one translation for nine rows, which is the exact failure it
exists to catch.

## Pass sixteen: the failure that says nothing

Changing channel on Kick is an app navigation: the URL moves, the chat is
remounted, the page never reloads. If the extension does not rebind, every
message after that is left alone, with no error and no reason on any line. It

the same as a positive one.

## Pass twenty-five: two words missing from a picture

The fabricated screenshots showed twelve messages and ten translations. Two
lines had none, and the counter in the bar agreed. Reading the reason the
product leaves on the line: "the translation came back the same as the
original", which is what my own mock returns when a sentence is not in its
phrasebook.

Instrumenting the mock said which sentences it could not find, and they were not
the sentences that had been typed. The extension had asked it to translate
`no me lo puedo creer otra lo mismo` and `que usas para el raton`. The words
`vez` and `configuracion` were gone before anything left the page.

`parseKickContent` strips inline emote names, and two of its three rules hit
ordinary language:

  - `\w+(...|gg|ez|...)\d*` deletes any word ending in `ez`. Turkish forms
    the negative aorist with `-mez`, so `etmez`, `istemez`, `gitmez`,
    `gerekmez`, `gondermez` all vanish. Turkish is the second most read of the
    localised listings. Spanish `vez`, Czech `bez` and `kez` go the same way.
  - `[a-z]{3,}[a-z0-9]{10,}` deletes any all-lowercase word of thirteen
    characters or more. Thirteen is an ordinary length in Spanish, Portuguese,
    Turkish, German and Finnish.

Measured over 11583 words of the store listings, which are the biggest real
multilingual corpus in the repository: 30 distinct ordinary words destroyed.
Split by rule, the mixed-case rule accounted for 13, all brand names, and caught
8 of 13 sampled emote names. The long-word rule accounted for 8 and caught
exactly one emote name, which the suffix rule already catches, so it bought
nothing. The suffix rule accounted for 9, every one of them from `ez`.

So: the long-word rule now requires a digit, and `ez` and `gg` leave the suffix
list. After, 13 words destroyed instead of 30, all of them brand names from the
rule that earns its keep, and emote coverage unchanged at 10 of 13, since
OMEGALUL is still caught by `lul` and namedarumajankiss2 by `kiss`.

Eight regression tests. Seven of them fail against the old rules. The ten parser
tests that already existed passed either way, which is why this lived here long
enough to reach production in eleven languages.

**On how it was found.** Not by a gate. By staging screenshots on a fabricated
page and looking at the picture, then refusing to accept "my mock is missing an
entry" without checking which entry.
